# ADR 0004 — Transpile the source's status tree; do not hand-transcribe it

Status: accepted, 2026-09-14.
Relationship: **supersedes the METHOD of `pikachu-moveset/docs/adr/0006`, keeps its principle.**

## The decision

Class-3 moves — those with their own status machine, or that capture another fighter — are
produced by a **Ghidra-C → smashline-Rust transpiler** over the source's decompiled status
functions. Not by a human transcribing them, and not by a curated library of hand-written moves.

The transpiler emits **generated Rust on disk**, regenerated by the build and never hand-edited.

## Relationship to the moveset context's ADR 0006

ADR 0006 says *transcribe the source's status tree; do not reimplement it*, and it was right: the
Alolan Whip and the cargo carry were scrapped and rewritten as line-by-line transcriptions after
seven rounds in which reimplementation-from-description fixed something real each time and left
the move broken a new way.

This ADR does not disagree with that. It **mechanises** it. The principle — the port is the
source's own logic, and the diff against the source is checkable — is unchanged. What changes is
who does the typing, and the reason is [ADR 0001](0001-a-tool-anyone-can-use.md): a human
transcribing 700 lines of Ghidra output is a workflow for one operator, not a product.

Two documents that look like they disagree are worse than one, so: **0006 governs the moveset,
this governs the builder, and the difference is the audience.**

## Why a transpiler is plausible at all

A status function is largely a straight sequence of engine calls with constants marshalled between
them. That is not a hope; it is why `disasm --trace` works — it drops the `L2CValue` constructor
and accessor traffic and yields about 45 lines of signal where the raw disassembly has 400 of
noise:

```
call LinkModule__set_model_constraint_pos_ort  [LINK_NO_CAPTURE, "rot", "throw",
                                                CONSTRAINT_FLAG_POSITION, CONSTRAINT_FLAG_ORIENTATION]
call WorkModule__get_param_int    ["param_special_s", "special_s_catch_clatter_frame"]
call ControlModule__start_clatter
```

Three things arrived recently that make the input tractable where it was not: **all 100,508
functions of `main` decompiled**, **all 8,792 functions of `lua2cpp_common`** — which is where
every victim-side status lives and which the public decomp does not contain — and the **constant
table fully resolved**, 19,310 of 19,310 offsets carrying a name *and* a value, so a
`ldr wN, [xTABLE, #0x250]` reads as `STATUS_KIND_NONE = 0xFFFFFFFF` statically.

## Why it has an unusually good oracle

The twelve hand-written status modules in `pikachu-moveset` work on hardware. Transpiling
Incineroar's side-B tree and diffing against the module that already works is a real acceptance
test, not a plausibility argument. If the transpiler reproduces a known-good module it is
trustworthy; if it cannot, the boundary is found for the cost of a week rather than discovered
later by a user.

## Why the mechanical part matters most

Across the roster, `init` appears in **1,269** statuses, `exec` in **1,205**, and `exit` in
**831** — 20–30% of all statuses have a line beyond the pre/main/main_loop/end quartet. The single
most expensive bug in this project's history was one of those: Donkey Kong's `SHOULDER_WALK` has
an `Exec` line, the port registered Init/Pre/Main/Exit and no Exec, and the carry walk moved
`+0.00` units while looking alive.

That is not bad luck at a 28% base rate, and it is **detectable by `ls`** — the decomp ships one
`.c` per line a status actually has. A transpiler that enumerates lines from the directory cannot
make that mistake; a human reading 700 lines of C can, and did.

## Considered and rejected

**A curated library** of hand-written class-3 moves, growing over time, refusing the rest. This was
the recommendation and it was overridden. It is the safe option and it makes the tool's hardest
capability depend on the author's personal throughput forever — a builder whose command grabs are
whatever one person has already built is a library with a CLI.

**An IR the plugin interprets at runtime.** Smaller output, no codegen, and smashline does export
a `smashline_interpreter` symbol so there is precedent. Rejected because **the entire verification
layer greps source text**: `check-callparity`, `check-statuslines`, `check-status` and
`check-keepflags` all work by reading the port's Rust. An IR means rewriting every gate, and it
hides exactly what this project's debugging method depends on — reading generated code beside the
source C and diffing them.

**Emitting an unimplemented stub** when a called helper has not been decompiled. Rejected for the
same reason as the `Exec` bug: a port that compiles and is silently partial is the failure mode the
completeness gates exist to prevent. A missing helper **refuses the move** and names the command
that would fetch it.

## Consequences

- The transpiler's output must be traceable back to the source C line it came from, or debugging a
  wrong port becomes worse than debugging a hand-written one.
- Coverage is uneven and must be surfaced: 95 fighters have community-decompiled status trees
  **without their unnamed `FUN_*` helpers**, and the helpers are where the interesting parts hid
  every previous time — the whip's whole kinetic core was one of them.
- A transpiled port is still not a *correct* port. It is a complete one. Correctness needs the
  in-game self-test and a playtest, and nothing here changes that.
